Highlight of the day

“Irradiance describes the sun’s momentary power; irradiation describes the energy collected over time.”

Key discussion: Solar Resource Fundamentals

What I learned

Irradiance is the amount of solar power arriving at a surface at a particular moment. It is measured in W/m². Irradiation, or insolation, is the accumulated solar energy over time, commonly measured in kWh/m².

When modelling a PV module, irradiance is used for momentary output predictions, while irradiation is used to estimate daily energy yield.

GHI, DNI & DHI

GHI — Global Horizontal Irradiance — is the total solar irradiance received on a horizontal surface. It includes both direct and diffuse radiation and is commonly used for flat-plate solar systems.

DNI — Direct Normal Irradiance — measures direct sunlight on a surface perpendicular to the sun’s rays. It is especially important for concentrating solar systems.

DHI — Diffuse Horizontal Irradiance — is sunlight scattered by the atmosphere, including air molecules, dust, water vapour, and pollutants, that reaches a horizontal surface.

Measurement & modelling

A pyranometer is a scientific sensor used to measure solar irradiance. It captures both direct sunlight and diffuse sky radiation.

Common types of pyranometers include thermopile and silicon photocell sensors. A detector beneath a glass dome converts sunlight into a small electrical signal proportional to its intensity.

The zenith angle, θz, is the angle between the sun and the vertical direction directly above an observer.

A sun-position diagram or simulation can track how the sun moves across the sky and show how panel tilt and orientation affect plane-of-array irradiance.

Peak sun hours

Peak Sun Hours represent the total solar energy received at a location during a day. They compress a day of changing irradiance into an equivalent number of hours at 1,000 W/m².

A location may have 12 hours of sunlight but only 5.5 peak sun hours because solar intensity changes throughout the day. PSH is useful for solar-system sizing and expected-energy calculations.

Application idea

Build an AI-assisted tracker that records daily sun hours and sky conditions, then predicts the following day. The project could also compare locations by maximum and expected solar energy using simulated sun position, GHI, DNI, DHI, panel orientation, and weather conditions.

Highlight of the day

“A transistor can switch or amplify — but underneath both jobs, it's just a gate controlling a much bigger flow.”

From Notes on Electronics

My note

A transistor is a semiconductor device that acts as a switch or an amplifier, boosting a small signal into a larger one. As a switch, a small voltage applied to the base pushes current into the emitter, and this current is controlled by, and flows through, the collector once the device enters its active region.

For a BC548 transistor, the base emitter junction needs about 0.7V to turn on and enter the active region. If the base voltage rises too far past this, the transistor enters breakdown and gets damaged. It helps to picture the transistor as two diodes joined together at the base, one at the base emitter junction and one at the base collector junction.

One relationship worth remembering: as Vcc increases, Vbb decreases, but both Ib and Ic increase gradually as a result.

Bipolar Junction Transistor

The BJT is a three terminal device that comes in two types: NPN (negative positive negative) and PNP (positive negative positive). The three terminals are called the base (B), collector (C) and emitter (E).

Calculations and formulas

In this section we are using a BC548 pinout transistor, where C is the collector, B is the base and E is the emitter.

The collector current and the base current are related by:

Ic = β × Ib

Applying Kirchhoff's Current Law at the transistor:

Ie = Ic + Ib
